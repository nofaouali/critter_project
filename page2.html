HABITAT


Roseate Spoonbills reside primarily in Southeast America, reaching parts of Mexico and South America. They linger by mangroves, willows, cypress, and other trees by the marshes. The birds consume aquatic invertebrates that wander in swamps. Some of their most prominent locations are the Everglades and Florida natural reserves. Evidently, roseate spoonbills reside primarily in wetland regions, by marshes and reeds—in fresh or saltwater vegetation. During the spring to autumn months, roseate spoonbills enjoy roaming the bays and lagoonsalong the Gulf Coast. Roseate spoonbills rest and build nest on trees, often in colonies of mutiple birds. Being wading birds, they are usually present when waters are shallow, remaining in regions inhabited additionally by egrets, herons, and ibises.

Migration

Roseate Spoonbills enjoy warm weather in the summertime, often finding refuge in coastal Texas. During the frigid winter months, the birds flock to parts of Mexico, Central or South America.
