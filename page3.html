SEVEN CHARACTERISTICS OF LIFE


Metabolism

Metabolism is an important part of the seven characteristics of life. It plays a role in how an organism can produce energy to survive. The roseate spoonbill can metabolize by consuming and digesting pigmented crustaceans to produce adenosine triphosphate, or ATP. Without metabolizing, they would eventually perish due to a lack of energy, vital for hunting and survival. Additionally, metabolism causes the bird to maintain their bright pink plumage.

Based on information from Texas Parks and Wildlife, “Spoonbills eat shrimp, shrimp eat algae, and the algae make their own red and yellow pigments, called carotenoids. Some scientists believe that the pink coloration that roseate spoonbills acquire as they mature is due to their diet of carotenoid-rich organisms like shrimp. The more they eat, the pinker they get.” The evidence displays that by using energy, roseate spoonbills slowly develop their color through metabolic processes.

Homeostasis

Homeostasis is an organism's ability to maintain or stabilize its internal system regardless of the impact of external conditions.For instance, roseate spoonbills can part their bill to decrease body temperature—similar to how a human would sweat. In addition, roseate spoonbills use a process called osmoregulation to regulate the salt and water balance. This is achieved by aspects like behaviors and unique characteristics.

According to PubMed Central, “The endangered…Spoonbill strictly breeds in marine environments and is threatened by the rapid loss of coastal wetlands within its breeding range. Adults with chicks are thought to gradually switch feeding sites from freshwater wetlands to coastal mudflats as the chicks’ osmoregulatory system develops. We investigated age-dependent shifts in the diet of [roseate] spoonbill chicks at four breeding colonies with varying freshwater habitat availability by examining stable isotopes (δ13C, δ15N) between the tip (grown at the age of 10 days) and middle (grown at the age of 22 days) portions of their primary feathers. The δ13C value of the middle portions was significantly higher than that of the tips, which suggested that the ratio of marine resources increased with the growth and development of chicks.” This evidence supports that roseate spoonbills use homeostasis due to the chicks’ inability to regulate the salt consumed through saltwater environments. Due to this, as their osmoregulation develops, juvenile roseate spoonbills and chicks rely on consuming solely in freshwater regions. All in all, roseate spoonbills display homeostasis by removing excess salt through glands as they mature.

Cellular Organization

An organism made of cells is essential, which classifies a living and a nonliving organism. Roseate Spoonbills are eukaryotic organisms, obtaining a nucleus containing DNA, transferable to offspring. According to Wikipedia: Mitosis: “The genome is composed of a number of chromosomes—complexes of tightly coiled DNA that contain genetic information vital for proper cell function.” This proves that roseate spoonbills have cells, specifically plant and animal cells that have a nucleus. This defines their ability to reproduce, metabolize, and generate cells—characteristics solely defined by a living thing.

Growth and Development

Roseate spoonbills portray growth and development through a variety of factors. Their bills slowly form into a flattened edge, their pale feathers eventually turn pink, and their eyes obtain a crimson hue as they age. Additionally, as they age, roseate spoonbills slowly lose the feathers on their heads and necks. These unveil aspects that define a roseate spoonbill’s maturity, reaching an average lifespan of about ten years in the wild. By growing, roseate spoonbills display a natural circumstance that sets the basis of their evolution. Furthermore, the colorful birds undergo short-term development to produce offspring early and limit the population in wetland regions as they grow old.

Response to Stimuli

Roseate spoonbills constantly adapt to their environments, an immense role that defines a living organism. Without reaction to stimuli, the birds would be unable to find resources or use homeostasis to survive in the wilderness. Moreover, roseate spoonbills respond to their environments by migrating during the wintry months to warmer regions. This example portrays how their bodies react to stimuli such as frigid weather or a change in the atmosphere. Also, with the effects of climate change, roseate spoonbills have proceeded to move north to adapt to the pivotal changes.

According to Unity College: Everglades Science Center, "Spoonbills in Florida Bay, however, have demonstrated significant adaptability to environmental changes. In response to water level changes from anthropogenic land-based alterations of water flow into Florida Bay, including canals and water pumps for land development, spoonbills nested later in the season and modified their nest locations (Porter et al., 2002).” This evidence supports how roseate spoonbills display the characteristic of life: reacting to their environment. They adapt to the alterations of water levels by changing their habitat. Evidently, these factors all contribute to how roseate spoonbills are living organisms.

Evolution

Roseate spoonbills have grown to adopt unique traits and characteristics over time. Some of these include their bills, which evolve to enable them to identify food.

According to Medcrave Online: Ecological factors shaping the evolution and behavior of spoonbills: insights for conservation and management, “These birds have undergone remarkable adaptations, particularly in their bill morphology, which plays a pivotal role in their feeding behavior. The unique spoon-shaped bill enables them to employ a specialized feeding technique known as "tactile feeding," where they sweep their bills from side to side through shallow water to detect and capture prey with remarkable precision.” This evidence portrays how roseate spoonbills’ offspring have evolved through facets like natural selection or breeding. Additionally, “The specialized bill morphology of spoonbills enables them to engage in filter-feeding, a feeding strategy that involves extracting small organisms from the water or mud. Their bills are equipped with sensitive nerve endings that allow them to detect prey through touch.” Roseate spoonbills use these evolved techniques to support their ability to consume and find prey. All of these aspects contribute to their definition of living organisms.

Reproduction

By reproducing, roseate spoonbills can continue to have offspring. This sustains the number of birds in the future and keeps the population’s continuity. By choosing a mate every season, a female roseate spoonbill will constantly build nests and hatch chicks. Additionally, a primary facet of the seven characteristics of life is reproduction without a host. Fertilization of an egg results in a hatched chick, demonstrating reproduction and supporting the roseate spoonbill’s status as a living organism.
